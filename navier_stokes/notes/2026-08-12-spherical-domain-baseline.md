# Spherical-domain baseline for the Navier–Stokes proof challenge

Date: 2026-08-12

Status: **MODEL / TESTBED**. This note fixes the first auxiliary geometry and diagnostics before any proof claim is attempted.

## 1. Scope separation

The Clay problem asks for global smoothness or breakdown for the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations on `R^3` or on the periodic space `R^3/Z^3`.

This project begins instead with a bounded spherical domain

\[
\Omega_R=B_R(0)=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x|<R\}.
\]

Therefore a theorem proved only on `B_R(0)` is not by itself a solution of the Millennium Prize Problem. Any later proof route must explicitly supply a transfer mechanism, such as estimates uniform in `R`, localization, or a justified `R\to\infty` passage.

Official problem description: Charles L. Fefferman, *Existence and Smoothness of the Navier–Stokes Equation*, Clay Mathematics Institute.

## 2. Baseline PDE

For `x∈Ω_R`, `t≥0`,

\[
\partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u
=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta u,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot u=0,
\qquad
\nu>0,
\]

with external forcing fixed to

\[
f\equiv0.
\]

For the closed spherical testbed the primary boundary condition is

\[
u|_{\partial\Omega_R}=0.
\]

A different object, an observation ball cut out of an `R^3` solution, must not be silently identified with this rigid-boundary problem.

## 3. Central excitation without a point singularity

The phrase “motion begins at the center” is implemented as a smooth excitation supported in a small central ball `B_ε(0)`, with `0<ε<R`, rather than as a delta-like point force.

Let `ψ∈C_c^∞(B_ε(0))`. For each Cartesian axis `a∈{x,y,z}`, define the solenoidal seed family

\[
u_0^{(a)}
=
\nabla\times\nabla\times\bigl(\psi e_a\bigr).
\]

Then

\[
\nabla\cdot u_0^{(a)}=0,
\]

and because the support stays away from `∂Ω_R`, the initial field is compatible with the no-slip boundary.

The three axis runs `x`, `y`, `z` form a rotational-symmetry control set. With a spherical domain and identically rotated initial data, their scalar diagnostics must agree up to rotation. Persistent differences are evidence of discretization or implementation anisotropy, not new physics or a theorem.

## 4. Cartesian and spherical decomposition

Record

\[
u=(u_x,u_y,u_z)
\]

and, for `r=|x|>0`,

\[
e_r=\frac{x}{|x|},
\qquad
u_r=u\cdot e_r,
\qquad
u_t=u-u_r e_r.
\]

This separates motion aligned with the coordinate axes from the radial/tangential redistribution seen on spherical shells.

## 5. A necessary incompressibility warning

A nontrivial smooth purely radial source flow from the origin is incompatible with incompressibility.

If

\[
u(x,t)=q(r,t)e_r,
\]

then

\[
\nabla\cdot u
=
\frac{1}{r^2}\partial_r\bigl(r^2q(r,t)\bigr)=0.
\]

Hence

\[
r^2q(r,t)=C(t).
\]

Smoothness at `r=0` forces `C(t)=0`, so the only globally smooth purely radial incompressible field of this form is the zero field.

Therefore “3D outward wave” is not implemented as net fluid creation at the center. It is measured as the radial redistribution of velocity, vorticity, pressure, and energy generated by a divergence-free central excitation.

## 6. Shell diagnostics

For `0<r<R`, define spherical shell averages.

### Energy density

\[
E(r,t)
=
\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}
\int_{|x|=r}\frac12|u(x,t)|^2\,dS.
\]

### Enstrophy density

With

\[
\omega=\nabla\times u,
\]

define

\[
W(r,t)
=
\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}
\int_{|x|=r}|\omega(x,t)|^2\,dS.
\]

### Pressure fluctuation

Let

\[
\bar p(r,t)
=
\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}
\int_{|x|=r}p(x,t)\,dS,
\]

and track angular deviations `p-\bar p` separately.

### Net radial flux

For every sphere centered at the origin,

\[
\Phi(r,t)
=
\int_{|x|=r}u\cdot n\,dS.
\]

A smooth divergence-free field with no internal source must satisfy

\[
\Phi(r,t)=0.
\]

Local outward and inward sectors may both exist, but their total flux cancels.

### Axis-resolved energy

Track

\[
E_i(r,t)
=
\frac{1}{4\pi r^2}
\int_{|x|=r}\frac12|u_i(x,t)|^2\,dS,
\qquad i\in\{x,y,z\}.
\]

This allows direct comparison between coordinate-axis transport and the full spherical response.

## 7. Meaning of “wave” in this track

The incompressible model does not contain an ordinary finite-speed acoustic wavefront. Pressure is coupled through the incompressibility constraint and viscosity produces diffusive smoothing.

Accordingly the baseline vocabulary is:

- **radial response / spherical-shell redistribution** for the incompressible track;
- **acoustic or finite-speed pressure wave** only in a future compressible track.

The two must remain separated in proof notes and code.

## 8. Proof-status discipline

Every later result must use one of the repository labels:

- **THEOREM (external)**
- **DERIVED LEMMA**
- **COMPUTATIONAL CHECK**
- **CONJECTURE / TARGET**
- **MODEL / TESTBED**
- **FAILED ROUTE**

No numerical regularity observed for finite `R`, finite mesh, or finite time is to be promoted to a global smoothness claim.

## 9. Planned progression

1. Verify the smooth divergence-free central seed symbolically and numerically.
2. Verify rotational equivalence of the `x/y/z` seed families.
3. Implement shell diagnostics `E`, `W`, pressure fluctuation, and `Φ`.
4. Separate coordinate-axis transport from radial/tangential redistribution.
5. Search for scale-invariant or `R`-uniform a priori bounds.
6. Only after such bounds exist, examine whether the bounded-ball results can transfer to the official `R^3` problem.
