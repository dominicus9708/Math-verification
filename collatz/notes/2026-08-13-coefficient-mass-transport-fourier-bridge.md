# Coefficient-survivor mass transport and the ternary--dyadic Fourier bridge

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: **exact transport identity + exact Fourier identity + full-`m=44` finite certificate through binary depth 26**. The identities are general finite-group statements. The numerical `m=44` contraction data are exact finite results, not an asymptotic Collatz proof.

## 1. Reduced dyadic coordinate

Every member of the recursively sufficient ternary core has

\[
N=4Y+3.
\]

At binary depth `L`, the relevant formation coordinate is therefore

\[
Y\pmod{2^{L-2}}.
\]

Let

\[
M:=2^{L-2}.
\]

A parent class `r mod M` has two dyadic children modulo `2M`:

\[
r,\qquad r+M.
\]

For the full `m=44` ternary block, let

\[
c_0(r),\ c_1(r)
\]

be the exact numbers of ternary selector assignments landing in those two children.

Put

\[
c(r):=c_0(r)+c_1(r),
\qquad
u(r):=c_0(r)-c_1(r).
\]

## 2. Coefficient-survival language

Let

\[
b_L:=\min\{q:3^q\ge2^L\}
=\lceil L\log_3 2\rceil.
\]

A depth-`L` dyadic class is a **coefficient survivor** if every prefix of its parity word satisfies

\[
q_j\ge b_j.
\]

Let `R_L` denote the set of surviving parent classes modulo `M`.

For `r in R_L`, let

\[
\beta_0(r),\beta_1(r)\in\{0,1\}
\]

indicate whether its two depth-`L+1` children survive.

If the Beatty barrier does not rise from `L` to `L+1`, every surviving parent has two surviving children.

If

\[
b_{L+1}=b_L+1,
\]

then a surviving parent has either two surviving children or exactly one surviving child. The one-child parents are precisely the boundary layer whose odd count at depth `L` equals `b_L`.

Define

\[
m(r):=\beta_0(r)+\beta_1(r),
\qquad
v(r):=\beta_0(r)-\beta_1(r).
\]

Thus on one-child parents,

\[
m(r)=1,\qquad v(r)=\pm1.
\]

## 3. Exact mass-transport identity

Define the ternary representative mass surviving through depth `L` by

\[
\boxed{
C_L:=\sum_{r\in R_L}c(r).
}
\]

At a barrier-rise step define the boundary mass

\[
\boxed{
D_L:=
\sum_{\substack{r\in R_L\\m(r)=1}}c(r).
}
\]

and the signed child-correlation term

\[
\boxed{
K_L:=
\sum_{\substack{r\in R_L\\m(r)=1}}
v(r)\,u(r).
}
\]

For a two-child parent all mass survives. For a one-child parent the surviving child mass is

\[
\frac{c(r)+v(r)u(r)}2.
\]

Summing gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
C_{L+1}
=C_L-\frac{D_L}{2}+\frac{K_L}{2}.
}
\]

This contains no approximation and no individual-start assumption.

## 4. Formation imbalance bound

Define the total dyadic child imbalance over surviving parents by

\[
\boxed{
U_L:=\sum_{r\in R_L}|u(r)|.
}
\]

Since `|v(r)|=1` on the one-child boundary,

\[
\boxed{|K_L|\le U_L.}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
C_{L+1}
\le
C_L-\frac{D_L-U_L}{2}.
}
\]

Consequently every barrier-rise step with

\[
\boxed{U_L<D_L}
\]

forces strict contraction of the whole ternary representative mass.

This separates the proof task into two channels:

1. **Beatty boundary channel:** make `D_L` large;
2. **ternary--dyadic mixing channel:** make `U_L` small.

No ordinary integer trajectory appears in this inequality.

## 5. Fourier identity for the imbalance channel

For the full child group

\[
G=\mathbb Z/(2M)\mathbb Z,
\]

write `c_x` for the ternary selector count in residue `x` and use the unnormalized discrete Fourier transform

\[
\widehat c(k)
:=
\sum_{x=0}^{2M-1}
c_x e^{-2\pi i kx/(2M)}.
\]

For odd `k`, pairing `x=r` and `x=r+M` gives

\[
\widehat c(k)
=
\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}
(c_r-c_{r+M})
e^{-\pi i kr/M}.
\]

The odd frequencies form an orthogonal Fourier basis on the `M` parent positions. Hence Parseval gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}|c_r-c_{r+M}|^2
=
\frac1M
\sum_{\substack{0\le k<2M\\k\ {m odd}}}
|\widehat c(k)|^2.
}
\]

By Cauchy--Schwarz,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=0}^{M-1}|c_r-c_{r+M}|
\le
\sqrt{
\sum_{k\ {m odd}}|\widehat c(k)|^2
}.
}
\]

The left side over all parents dominates `U_L`, so this is a valid global upper bound for the formation-imbalance channel.

## 6. Exact ternary Fourier product

For selector depth `d`, the distribution is generated by

\[
Y
=3^{44}+\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_i3^i,
\qquad a_i\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The fixed `3^44` term contributes only a unit complex phase. Therefore, after normalization by `2^d`,

\[
\boxed{
|\widehat\mu_d(k)|
=
\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}
\left|
\cos\left(\frac{\pi k3^i}{2M}\right)
\right|.
}
\]

For the full `m=44` block, `d=44`.

Thus the quantity that can spoil ideal half-child contraction is a pure finite-group Fourier product of the ternary selector set.

The Collatz coefficient language enters only through the independent boundary set defining `D_L`.

## 8. Fourier--Cauchy diagnostic margin

Using the exact Fourier product of Section 6 and numerical high-precision summation as a diagnostic, the much weaker Cauchy upper bound still stays below the exact boundary mass at every barrier rise through `L=25`.

At the worst tested step `L=25`,

\[
\frac{U_L^{\rm Fourier-Cauchy}}{D_L}
\approx0.03535<1.
\]

This numerical Fourier evaluation is **not** the finite certificate in Section 7; Section 7 uses exact integer counts. Its role is to show that the analytic Fourier route has substantial room before needing the exact child counts.

## 9. Boundary-language diagnostic

If ternary mass were exactly uniform across the surviving dyadic classes, `D_L/C_L` would equal the purely combinatorial Beatty-ballot boundary-class ratio.

An odd-count-only ballot DP shows that at barrier-rise steps through `L=2000` this class ratio remains positive; the smallest observed value is approximately

\[
0.0753.
\]

This is a diagnostic, not a proved uniform lower bound.

The finite full-`m=44` weighted ratios through `L=25` closely track the corresponding class ratios, consistent with the very small measured formation imbalance.

## 10. New structural proof target

The mass-transport identity identifies a sharper growing-resolution target than raw survivor enumeration.

A sufficient structural route is to prove, on the relevant growing sequence of barrier-rise depths,

\[
\boxed{
D_L-U_L\ge \delta_L C_L
}
\]

with a positive sequence `delta_L` satisfying enough cumulative divergence, for example

\[
\sum_L\delta_L=\infty.
\]

Then

\[
C_{L+1}
\le
\left(1-\frac{\delta_L}{2}\right)C_L
\]

at every rise step, forcing the dangerous ternary mass downward without inspecting individual starts.

The two subproblems are now mathematically separated:

- obtain a ballot-theoretic lower bound for the boundary fraction;
- obtain a Fourier/spectral upper bound for ternary child imbalance.

This is precisely the desired set/proposition/static-aggregation architecture: the full candidate family is transported by two aggregate channels instead of enumerated integer by integer.
