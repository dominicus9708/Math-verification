# Mixed-interaction reduction for first coefficient crossing

Status: **DERIVED LEMMA + COMPUTATIONAL CHECK + NEXT TARGET**

We use the accelerated map

\[
T(n)=\begin{cases}
n/2,&n\text{ even},\\
(3n+1)/2,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]

At a fixed coefficient-stopping level, write

\[
q=n+1,\qquad \sigma=\lceil q\log_2 3\rceil,
\]
\[
M=2^\sigma,\qquad P=3^q,\qquad D=M-P>0.
\]

For an admissible odd-position vector

\[
\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_q),
\]

define

\[
R(\alpha)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}3^{q-i}2^{\alpha_i}.
\]

The canonical start/end pair \((x,y)\) satisfies

\[
My=Px+R,
\]

and with descent margin \(z=x-y\),

\[
\boxed{Dx-Mz=R.}
\]

## 1. Mixed-difference identity

The correction is additively separable in the coordinates \(\alpha_i\):

\[
R(\alpha)=\sum_i R_i(\alpha_i).
\]

Hence every mixed finite difference involving at least two distinct coordinates vanishes:

\[
\Delta_{i_1}\cdots\Delta_{i_m}R=0,\qquad m\ge2.
\]

Applying the same mixed difference to \(Dx-Mz=R\) gives

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{i_1}\cdots\Delta_{i_m}z
=\frac{D}{M}
\Delta_{i_1}\cdots\Delta_{i_m}x,
\qquad m\ge2.
}
\]

Therefore all off-diagonal / higher interaction tensors of the canonical-start field \(x\) and descent-margin field \(z\) are positively proportional. Their discrepancy is entirely first-order/additive.

This is stronger and more precise than a Monge conjecture: ordinary Monge inequalities can fail, while the mixed-difference signs of \(x\) and \(z\) are forced to agree wherever the full Cartesian cell is admissible.

## 2. One-coordinate shift and the dangerous-axis criterion

Shift one admissible odd position left by one step while all other coordinates are fixed. At coordinate \(i\), with the shifted position equal to \(a\), the correction change has magnitude

\[
c=3^{q-i}2^a.
\]

Let \(u\in(0,M)\) be the canonical start-residue increment satisfying

\[
Pu\equiv c\pmod M.
\]

Because \(P=3^q\),

\[
u\equiv 2^a3^{-i}\pmod M,
\]

and \(v_2(u)=a\).

If the canonical representative does not wrap modulo \(M\), \(x\) and \(z\) move in the same direction automatically.

If it wraps, a sufficient condition for the same co-ordering is

\[
D>3^{q-i}.
\]

Thus the only coordinate axes capable of producing an elementary \(x\)-vs-\(z\) order reversal satisfy

\[
3^{q-i}\ge D.
\]

Define the **dangerous interaction dimension**

\[
\boxed{
h(q)=\#\{1\le i\le q:3^{q-i}\ge D\}.
}
\]

Equivalently, if

\[
\delta_q=\sigma-q\log_2 3\in(0,1),
\]

then

\[
D=3^q(2^{\delta_q}-1)
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
h(q)=\max\!\left(0,\,q-\lceil\log_3 D\rceil\right).
}
\]

The dangerous dimension is therefore controlled by the Diophantine near-resonance between \(2^\sigma\) and \(3^q\), not directly by the nominal parity length.

## 4. Rigorous coarse dimension bound from the Ellison bound

Rozier–Terracol, using Ellison, record for \(q\ge13\):

\[
|2^j-3^q|>\frac{2.56^q}{2}.
\]

At a coefficient crossing, \(D=2^\sigma-3^q\), hence every dangerous axis obeys

\[
3^{q-i}\ge D>\frac{2.56^q}{2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
i<\left(1-\log_3 2.56\right)q+\log_3 2,
\]

where

\[
1-\log_3 2.56\approx0.1443685200,
\qquad
\log_3 2\approx0.6309297536.
\]

So the number of axes that can even *locally* reverse start-order versus descent-margin order is at most about 14.44% of \(q\) under this coarse unconditional bound.

This does **not** yet prove the Hierarchical Extremal Principle globally; the admissible set is constrained and a global order reversal could involve a path of multiple coordinate moves. What is proved is narrower:

> any elementary order-reversal edge must occur in the first \(h(q)\) coordinate axes.

## 5. Transfer-matrix / tensor formulation

Use slack coordinates

\[
s_i=\kappa(i-1)-\alpha_i\ge0,
\qquad
\kappa(i)=\lfloor i\log_2 3\rfloor.
\]

Let

\[
d_i=\kappa(i)-\kappa(i-1)\in\{1,2\}.
\]

Strict increase of the odd positions gives the sparse transition rule

\[
s_{i+1}\le s_i+d_i-1.
\]

Hence the admissible family is generated by sparse lower-triangular transfer matrices

\[
(A_i)_{s,t}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\le s+d_i-1.
\]

\[
w_i(s)=3^{q-i}2^{\kappa(i-1)-s}.
