# R1 gap44 + depth27 uniform mixed-fibre bound

Date: 2026-08-14

Status: **exact finite mixed-place theorem at the current R1 resonance**. It combines the final-44 odd-event renewal-gap fibre with the independently certified depth-27 binary/Hensel hard-core on the same m44 ternary selector family. For every fixed final-44 suffix address, at most `134,940` of the full `2^44` m44 selectors can satisfy both constraints. No probabilistic independence assumption is used. This does not globalize over all suffix addresses and does not prove Collatz.

## 2. Low/high ternary decomposition at scale 3^21

Write

\[
S=3^{21}T+L,
\]

with

\[
T\in C_{23},
\qquad
L\in C_{21}.

Thus

\[
\boxed{

The general Cantor interval lemma shows that an interval of length `<3^21` meets at most two consecutive copies in this decomposition. Therefore every fixed suffix fibre is contained in at most two partial low-21 copies.

For the uniform upper bound it is enough to bound the number of depth-27 retained selectors in one **complete** low-21 copy and then double it.

## 5. Uniform fixed-suffix mixed bound

A gap44 suffix interval of length `<3^21` meets at most two low-21 Cantor copies.

For either copy, even if the whole copy rather than only the actual tail/head subset is retained, the depth-27 Hensel condition leaves at most

\[
67,470
\]

selectors.

Therefore every fixed final-44 suffix address satisfies the rigorous uniform bound

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{m44 selectors compatible with suffix + depth27 Hensel}\}
\le
2\cdot67,470
=
134,940.
}
\]

Relative to the full selector family,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{134,940}{2^{44}}
<7.671\times10^{-9}.
}
\]

Equivalently the fibre compression exceeds

\[
\boxed{2^{26.95}}.

This is stronger than either input alone:

- depth27 alone: approximately `3.16%` retained at dyadic resolution;
- exact common-selector intersection: at most `134,940` per fixed suffix.

No multiplication of statistical percentages is used. The maximum is taken over **all dyadic shifts**, so the theorem is uniform in the high ternary prefix and in the position of the fixed suffix interval.

## 6. Meaning and remaining obstruction

This is the first current-R1 theorem in the project where

1. the final odd-event suffix fixes a `3`-adic renewal-gap fibre;
2. the initial ordinary start must simultaneously lie in a binary/Hensel minimality hard-core;
3. the two constraints are intersected on the same ternary selector assignments by an exact group correlation.

The remaining obstruction is no longer the size of one suffix fibre. A fixed fibre is now tiny.

The missing global step is to control the **number and overlap of genuinely realizable final-44 suffix fibres when the same candidate must also carry the global phase/skew/defect and dyadic-address state**.

The record condition alone cannot provide this control because the suffix-unit-surjectivity theorem allows all local unit residues. The next state must therefore retain a global channel—most naturally the skew/defect cost or the same-integer dyadic renewal address—across the Euclidean scale.
