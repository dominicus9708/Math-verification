# R1 gap44 + depth27 uniform mixed-fibre bound

Date: 2026-08-14

Status: **exact finite mixed-place theorem at the current R1 resonance**. It combines the final-44 odd-event renewal-gap fibre with the independently certified depth-27 binary/Hensel hard-core on the same m44 ternary selector family. For every fixed final-44 suffix address, at most `134,265` of the full `2^44` m44 selectors can satisfy both constraints. No probabilistic independence assumption is used. This does not globalize over all suffix addresses and does not prove Collatz.

## 1. Inputs

\[

The companion gap44 theorem gives, for one fixed final-44 suffix address, a cyclic interval `I` of length `<3^21` such that the full selector coordinate

## 2. Low/high ternary decomposition at scale 3^21

Write

\[
S=3^{21}T+L,
\]

with

\[
T\in C_{23},
\qquad
L\in C_{21}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
C_{44}
=
\bigsqcup_{T\in C_{23}}
\left(3^{21}T+C_{21}\right).
}
\]

The general Cantor interval lemma shows that an interval of length `<3^21` meets at most two copies in this decomposition.

If it meets two copies, their high-prefix starts must be **consecutive at distance exactly `3^21`**. Indeed, the convex-hull width of one `C_21` copy is `(3^21-1)/2`; copies whose starts differ by at least `2*3^21` are separated by more than `3^21` even after accounting for that width.

Hence the two-copy case has a fixed relative shift, not two arbitrary independent shifts.

## 3. Reduced dyadic coordinate

Modulo `2^27`,

\[

\[
\boxed{

\in\mathcal A
\pmod{2^{25}}.

For one fixed high-prefix value `T`, put

\[
h:=3^{44}+3^{21}T\pmod{2^{25}}.
\]

The number of retained low selectors in that whole copy is

\[
\boxed{
C(h)
:=
\sum_{x\in\mathbb Z/2^{25}\mathbb Z}
L_{21}(x)\,1_{\mathcal A}(x+h),

where

\[
L_{21}(x)
:=
\#\left\{
(a_0,\ldots,a_{20})\in\{0,1\}^{21}:
\sum_{i=0}^{20}a_i3^i\equiv x\pmod{2^{25}}
\right\}.
\]

The certificate evaluates `C(h)` for **every** `h mod 2^25`, so the resulting bound is uniform in the actual high ternary prefix.

## 5. Exact adjacent-two-copy maximum

If the suffix interval meets two low-21 copies, their ternary starts differ by `3^21`. Therefore their reduced dyadic shifts differ by

\[
\boxed{
\Delta
:=3^{21}\bmod2^{25}
=24,924,851.
}
\]

The same exact correlation array therefore gives the complete-copy overestimate

\[
C(h)+C(h+\Delta).
\]

The NTT certificate evaluates this for every `h mod 2^25` and finds

\[
\boxed{
\max_h\bigl(C(h)+C(h+\Delta)\bigr)
=134,265.
}
\]

The maximum occurs at

\[
\boxed{h=26,369,234}.
\]

The actual suffix interval uses only a **tail** of the first copy and a **head** of the second, so replacing those partial copies by both complete copies is a safe overestimate. Hence `134,265` is a rigorous uniform fixed-suffix bound.

## 6. Uniform mixed-fibre theorem

For every fixed final-44 odd-event suffix address,

\[
\boxed{
\#\{\text{m44 selectors compatible with suffix + depth27 Hensel}\}
\le134,265.
}
\]

Relative to the full selector family,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{134,265}{2^{44}}
<7.633\times10^{-9}.
}
\]

Thus the fixed-suffix mixed compression factor exceeds roughly

\[
\boxed{2^{26.96}}.
\]

For comparison:

- gap44 alone: at most `2^21 = 2,097,152` selectors per fixed suffix;
- one full low-21 copy after depth27 Hensel: at most `67,470`;
- exact two-adjacent-copy uniform overestimate: `134,265`.

No product of heuristic percentages is used. Both constraints act on the same ternary selector assignments, and the maximum is taken over the full dyadic shift group.

## 7. Remaining obstruction

A fixed final-44 suffix fibre is now extremely small. The unresolved R1 problem is therefore not a per-fibre counting problem.

The missing global step is to control the number and overlap of **genuinely realizable suffix fibres** after retaining information not present in the local record condition.

The record condition alone cannot do this because the suffix-unit-surjectivity theorem realizes every local unit residue. The next state must carry at least one genuinely global channel, such as

- skew/defect cost;
- strengthened same-integer dyadic renewal address;
- or a growing Euclidean phase/minimal-predecessor state.

This theorem narrows the quantitative target: any global suffix-state compression that reduces the effective number of mutually distinct fibres below about

\[
\frac{2^{44}}{134,265}
\approx1.31\times10^8
\]

would already force substantial overlap pressure on the m44 selector core. Complete exclusion still requires more than this counting threshold, but the scale of the remaining suffix-state problem is now explicit.
