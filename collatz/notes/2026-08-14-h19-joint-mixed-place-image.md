# Exact joint mixed-place image of the hard neutral `H_19` fibre

Date: 2026-08-14

Status: **exact finite joint-image theorem**.  This note demonstrates on the full neutral hard block that large marginal 3-adic and dyadic freedom does not imply large simultaneous freedom.  It is the finite prototype of the required gate-scale mixed-place boundary invariant.  It does not prove Collatz.

## 1. Neutral fibre

Let

\[
H_{19}=1101101101011011010
\]

and let

\[
\mathcal F_0
=
\{w:(\Sigma,M)=(0,0)\text{ relative to }H_{19}\}.
\]

Exact enumeration gives

\[
\boxed{|\mathcal F_0|=2652.}
\]

Every member has odd count

\[
q=12.
\]

For `u,v in F_0`, let

\[
D=R_u-R_v.
\]

Every neutral orientation begins with the same mandatory first two odd symbols, so

\[
4\mid D.
\]

Define the normalized exact difference

\[
\boxed{d:=D/4.}
\]

## 2. Exact difference set

Enumerating all ordered fibre pairs and deduplicating the exact integer differences gives

\[
\boxed{
|\{d:u,v\in\mathcal F_0\}|
=308,669.
}
\]

The exact normalized difference interval is

\[
\boxed{-260337\le d\le260337.}
\]

Thus the whole mixed-place image below is generated by only 308,669 actual integer differences.

## 3. Ternary marginal

For every

\[
1\le J\le10,
\]

the ternary projection is full:

\[
\boxed{
\{d\bmod3^J\}
=
\mathbb Z/3^J\mathbb Z.
}
\]

In particular at `J=10`, all

\[
\boxed{3^{10}=59,049}
\]

Hensel targets occur.

## 4. Dyadic marginal

The canonical block-start difference is

\[
\rho_u-\rho_v
\equiv
-3^{-12}D
\pmod{2^{19}}.
\]

After dividing the common factor four, use the normalized 17-bit coordinate

\[
\boxed{
y(d):=-3^{-12}d\pmod{2^{17}}.}
\]

Multiplication by the odd unit `-3^-12` only permutes dyadic residues.  Exact enumeration gives

\[
\boxed{
|\{y(d)\}|=131,047
}
\]

out of

\[
2^{17}=131,072.
\]

Thus the dyadic marginal fills about

\[
\boxed{99.9809265\%}
\]

of the normalized address space.

## 6. Marginal freedom versus joint sparsity

At `J=10` the two marginals are individually almost maximally flexible:

\[
\boxed{
\text{ternary marginal}=100\%,
}
\]

\[
\boxed{

Yet the simultaneous image occupies only

\[
\boxed{

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{large 3-adic marginal freedom}
+

\not\Rightarrow

This is the exact finite phenomenon needed by the proof architecture.

## 7. Relation to earlier results

The companion difference-surjectivity theorem showed that the hard neutral fibre can repair every ten-digit Hensel target.  The fixed-Hensel kernel theorem showed that dyadic freedom collapses as more Hensel digits are fixed.

The present joint-image calculation unifies those statements:

- every ternary target exists somewhere;
- almost every normalized dyadic target exists somewhere;
- but very few **ordered pairs of targets** occur in one and the same correction difference.

Thus the correct boundary question is membership in `G_J`, not membership in either marginal projection.
