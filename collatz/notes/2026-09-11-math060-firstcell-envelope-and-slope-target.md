## 3. Rigorous continued-fraction certification

The certificate evaluates `log(3/2)` and `log 2` by the positive atanh series

\[
\log x
=2\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^{2n+1}}{2n+1},
\qquad
z=\frac{x-1}{x+1},
\]

with an explicit positive geometric tail bound. Therefore the continued-fraction decisions below are made from rational intervals, not floating point.

Two consecutive convergents of

\[

are certified as

\[
\frac{3,853,041,921}{6,586,818,670},

\[
6,586,818,670+65,470,613,321

Thus the first-cell pair `(d_0,q_0)` is the mediant of these consecutive convergents.

## 4. Two-block Denjoy-Koksma bound

Use the classical Denjoy-Koksma inequality for irrational rotation by `theta` and the periodic bounded-variation function

\[
f(x)=2^{-x},\qquad 0\le x<1.
\]

Its circle total variation is

\[
\operatorname{Var}(f)=1
\]

(the monotone drop contributes `1/2` and the periodic jump contributes `1/2`), while

\[
\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx=\frac1{2\log2}.
\]

For a convergent denominator `q_n`, Denjoy-Koksma gives

\[
\left|
\sum_{j=0}^{q_n-1}f(x+j\theta)
-\frac{q_n}{2\log2}
\right|\le1
\]

for every starting phase `x`.

Split the `q_0` terms into the two consecutive-convergent blocks above. The second block may start at an arbitrary rotated phase, which is allowed by the theorem. Therefore

\[
\left|
\sum_{n=0}^{q_0-1}\Omega_n
-\frac{q_0}{2\log2}
\right|\le2,
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
S_{\partial}(q_0)
\le
\frac{q_0}{6\log2}+\frac23.
}
\]

The standard theorem statement used here is, for example, Proposition 4.2 in the Springer paper *Multiple mixing for a class of conservative surface flows* (Inventiones Mathematicae), which states the bounded-variation Birkhoff-sum error `<= Var(f)` at continued-fraction denominators.

Reference: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00222-015-0596-6

A second accessible statement appears in ENS lecture notes, Theorem 35.

Reference: https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~sghazoua/cdln.pdf

## 8. Correct Bellman formulation

MATH-059 shows that requiring every edge to satisfy

\[
\mathcal P_e-\lambda_*\ell_e\ge0
\]

is too strong: a legal long edge violates such an edgewise test but is transient.

The correct target is to construct a future-complete state quotient and a potential `H` satisfying

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal P_e-\lambda_*\ell_e
+H(s')-H(s)\ge0
}
\]

on all legal long-path transitions, with endpoint/potential overhead controlled inside the available additive constant.

On a cycle, the potential telescopes, so this is equivalent to excluding a coefficient-valid cycle with mean penalty below `19/503` in a finite exact quotient, or to the corresponding well-founded Bellman inequality if the quotient remains infinite.
