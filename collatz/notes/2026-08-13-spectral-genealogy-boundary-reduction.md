# Spectral genealogy: reduction of survivor Fourier coefficients to Beatty boundary spectra

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: **exact algebraic consequence** of the spectral-renewal identities. It shows that every nonzero coefficient-survivor Fourier coefficient is generated by a finite history of ordinary and oriented Beatty-boundary transforms. Therefore the survivor spectrum need not be treated as an independent analytic object.

## 1. Renewal identities

Let `f_L=1_{R_L}` be the coefficient-survivor indicator at reduced modulus

\[
M_L=2^{L-2}.
\]

Let `B_L` be the one-child boundary at a rise step and `w_L` its anti-periodic child orientation.

Write

\[
I_L=
\begin{cases}
1,&b_{L+1}=b_L+1,\\
0,&b_{L+1}=b_L.
\end{cases}
\]

The exact renewal formulas are

\[
\widehat f_{L+1}(2t)
=2\widehat f_L(t)
-I_L\widehat{1_{B_L}}(t),
\]

and, for odd child frequency `u`,

\[
\widehat f_{L+1}(u)
=
\begin{cases}
0,&I_L=0,\\
\frac12\widehat w_L(u),&I_L=1.
\end{cases}
\]

## 2. Follow one frequency down its 2-adic ancestry

Take a nonzero frequency at depth `L` and write

\[
\boxed{k=2^s u,\qquad u\text{ odd}.}
\]

Put

\[
n_0:=L-s.
\]

Iterating the even-frequency recurrence from depth `n_0` through depth `L` gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f_L(2^s u)
=
2^s\widehat f_{n_0}(u)
-
\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}
2^{s-1-j}
I_{n_0+j}
\widehat{1_{B_{n_0+j}}}(2^j u).
}
\]

This is an exact finite identity.

## 3. The root odd frequency is itself a boundary object

The frequency `u` is odd at depth `n_0`.

Therefore the previous spectral-renewal step gives

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f_{n_0}(u)=0
}
\]

if the transition `n_0-1 -> n_0` is a plateau, while at a rise

\[
\boxed{
\widehat f_{n_0}(u)
=rac12
\widehat w_{n_0-1}(u).
}
\]

Thus the first term in the genealogy is either zero or an oriented-boundary transform.

## 4. Boundary-only generation theorem

Combining Sections 2--3:

> Every nonzero Fourier coefficient of the coefficient-survivor indicator is a finite linear combination of
> 
> 1. oriented-boundary Fourier coefficients `widehat w_m` at an odd root frequency;
> 2. ordinary boundary-indicator coefficients `widehat 1_{B_m}` along the subsequent even-frequency ancestry.

No independent spectral source exists.

In symbolic form,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Spec}(R_L)\setminus\{0\}
\subset
\operatorname{span}
\bigl(
\operatorname{Spec}(B_m),
\operatorname{Spec}(w_m):m<L
\bigr).
}
\]

The statement is about generation of Fourier coefficients, not set inclusion of frequency supports.

## 5. Consequence for spectral-exception splitting

The generic exception-splitting lemma originally asks for exceptional-frequency control of three normalized transforms:

\[
\rho_{R_L},
\qquad
\rho_{B_L},
\qquad
\rho_{w_L}.
\]

The genealogy shows that `rho_R` is not fundamentally independent. A sufficiently uniform set of estimates for the boundary transforms across the relevant ancestral depths automatically controls survivor exceptional coefficients as well.

Thus the near-linear cross-base analytic target reduces to two boundary objects:

\[
\boxed{
\text{ordinary Beatty boundary }1_{B_L}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{oriented Beatty boundary }w_L.
}
\]

## 6. Relation to plateau resets

If the odd root frequency lands immediately after a plateau, the root term is **exactly zero**.

Hence many high-valuation frequencies have genealogies that begin with no inherited survivor spectrum at all. Only boundary terms generated after that reset can contribute.

This gives a structural explanation for spectral complementarity:

- the ternary selector channel has arithmetic exceptional frequencies;
- the coefficient-survivor channel repeatedly forgets newly exposed odd spectrum at plateaus;
- all regrowth is forced through a smaller boundary layer.

## 7. Next theorem target

A block estimate of the form

\[
\max_{k\in\mathcal E_L}
\left(
|\rho_{B_L}(k)|+|\rho_{w_L}(k)|
\right)
\le e^{-cL}
\]

for the structured selector-exception family `E_L`, together with analogous bounds along the finite spectral genealogy, would suffice to control the survivor transform there.

This is materially narrower than proving a uniform spectral gap for the entire coefficient-survival language.
